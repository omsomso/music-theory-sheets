Reading the rows

  • Fingers are written going up. Coming down, play the same fingers in reverse.
  • For two or more octaves, repeat the pattern. Only the very first and last notes may change finger (a 5 becomes the thumb).
  • The thumb never lands on a black key in any scale on this sheet.

Patterns worth memorising

  • C, G, D, A and E major all use the same fingers.
  • Each hand uses finger 4 once per octave. Learn that note (Finger 4 column) and the rest falls into place.
  • In every flat major key, right-hand 4 plays B♭.

Left hand, 3 or 4? The chord's 3rd takes 3 only when it's a major 3rd on a black key (D, A, E, B, F♯ major). Everywhere else it takes 4: a minor 3rd is narrow enough for 5 and 4, and on white keys 4 keeps the hand in its normal shape. Going up past the octave, that finger crosses over the thumb, and 3 makes the wider reach to a black key easier.

Arpeggio fingerings follow Robert Kelley's standard arpeggio chart.

Block chords

triads, 7th chords, sus, add9
  • Right thumb on the bottom note, left thumb on the top note.
  • Notes a step apart get neighbouring fingers.
  • For the middle note, use 3. Switch to 2 when the wide gap (a 4th) is on the little-finger side.
  • The shape decides the fingers, not the key. These work for major, minor, diminished and augmented chords alike.
